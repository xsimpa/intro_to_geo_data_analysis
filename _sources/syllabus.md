## Learning outcomes

After successful completion of the course, you will:
- be able to read and write programs in the Python programming language.
- be able to read commonly used data formats in geosciences (csv), process the data for simple analyses (averages, quality filter), and plot the results of your analyses.
- know how to install a programming environment with appropriate additional packages on your own computer, and use it to solve common programming tasks in geosciences.
- use appropriate packages to process and visualise data;
- apply programming skills to the statistical analysis of geoscientific data.

Most importantly: **you will be able to expand and deepen your programming skills independently**!

## Prerequisites

The targeted audience for this lecture are **students at the bachelor level without previous experience in programming**. A background in earth science (introduction lecture) is desireable, with some knowledge of math and physics, and interest for scientific analysis and number crunching!

## Organisation of the class

The class is taught in English. But it is okay to ask questions in German!

This class follows the [flipped classroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flipped_classroom) model. You will acquire new knowledge at home by reading online materials (and watching videos when appropriate). This will allow to use the time together in class more efficiently to discuss the materials and write code.

<br>

The semester is 15 weeks long. **Each week will always be organised as such**:

- **On Tuesdays (10h15-11h45):** We begin the lecture with a short assessment to check if you understood the materials that you will have reviewed at home correctly. Based on your own assessment, you choose the topics and questions you would like to discuss in class. I can delve deeper into specific topics, or present small exercises to help reinforce the key concepts. At the end, you’ll receive instructions on the materials to read or watch for the following week. The lecture will then finish with an exercise.


```{important}
The class grants you 3.5 ECTS if successfully passed: in theory, this represents 6 to 8 hours work per week (not including holidays). For this course, it means that you will spend at least twice as much time doing homework than sitting in class.

I strongly recommend to work regularly for the class. Programming is quite different to other disciplines, and "doing nothing for a few months" cannot be replaced by a "no-sleep-48-hours-push" at the end of semester. Programming is a bit like learning how to ski or climb: it is best learned by doing, and you will notice that regular practice will make you better each week.
```
